Scanimate Lab

Flicker warning: moving, high-contrast, colour-cycling images can trigger discomfort or seizures in some people. Pause if unsure.

Draw a line with the mouse. It becomes a signal (0 at the bottom, 1 at the top) that loops over the duration set on the module. Connect it to any parameter in the rack. Below is its spectrum (FFT, mean removed): smooth curves put energy at low frequency, jagged curves spread it up.

Paint a spectrogram. Left to right is time (about 3 s, looping), bottom to top is frequency (100 Hz to 8 kHz, log scale). Press Play and the picture is turned into sound with an inverse STFT (inverse FFT per column, windowed, overlap-added). The same picture also gives three signals (low, mid, high energy at the playhead) for the rack. Right mouse button or Erase removes. Sound is auto-levelled so it never clips.

This is the 2D spectrum (FFT) of the picture above, live. Centre = slow changes, edges = fine detail, angle = direction of stripes. Paint on it: black areas are removed, then an inverse FFT shows the filtered image. The brush is point-symmetric so the result stays a real image. Average brightness is always kept. The filter works on a 256 by 256 copy of the image. Turn Mask on to see it in the main view.

Paint a program with colours. Each colour is a block. Touching cells of the same colour make one region (one block). Regions that touch get wired: sources feed shapes and numbers, shapes feed transformers, transformers feed Render (white). A region's size sets one parameter (listed in the report). These are my own simple rules, not the Piet language. Click Compile and the rack is replaced by what you painted (Undo brings the old one back). Left mouse paints, right mouse erases.
